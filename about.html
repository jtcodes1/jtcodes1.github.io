CHAPTER 01 / CURIOSITY

It started with Minesweeper.

I used to sneak downstairs to play Minesweeper on my dad’s old IBM PC. I knew how the game worked, but I was just as interested in what the computer was doing when I clicked. Later I got into building PCs, fixing electronics, and rebuilding Nintendo systems. Learning to program gave me a way to figure some of that out for myself.

I still approach problems pretty much the same way. I want to understand what’s actually happening, find the problem, and see if I can fix it.

CHAPTER 02 / BUILDING A FOUNDATION

Working my way through school.

I worked night shifts to pay for school, and there were times when I could afford only one class at a time. I eventually finished my associate degree with honors, earned Oakton’s President’s Scholar recognition five times, and transferred to DePaul.

I’m now a computer science senior at DePaul with a 4.0 GPA, three Dean’s List terms, a $20,000/year Transfer Merit Scholarship, and an expected graduation date of June 2027. I’ve become especially interested in numerical methods, systems engineering, and high-performance computing because a better algorithm can make a much bigger problem possible to tackle.

CHAPTER 03 / LOS ALAMOS

How I got into scientific computing.

In 2025, I was selected as 1 of 11 students for a Texas A&M undergraduate research program and started working with Los Alamos National Laboratory. I knew computer science, but computational materials science was new to me. That experience revealed the challenge I now work on: developing faster numerical methods for long-range charge response while preserving the physics of molecular simulations.

With Dr. Anders Niklasson’s mentorship in T-1, I went from building charge models to deriving GPU solvers and inventing ALAMOS and ALAMOS-0. I’m also first author on our nonlinear shadow molecular dynamics paper, available on arXiv, developed with coauthors at Los Alamos and Sandia National Laboratories. I ran the Langevin simulations and calculated the infrared spectra used to check whether the approximations held up. I'm now preparing my second first-author ALAMOS methods manuscript. Designing new algorithms, making them work in production-oriented HPC software, and rigorously validating the physics is the research direction I want to pursue in a Computer Science PhD.

CHAPTER 04 / BEYOND CODE

Outside of school and research.

I’ve volunteered at Saint Benedict in Niles for more than a decade, usually about eight hours a week, helping residents and training newer volunteers. I’ve also tutored 14 high-school students in introductory programming, worked as a top contributor at How-To Geek, spent five summers at camps across five different states (becoming Head Counselor at River Way Ranch Camp).

When I’m not working on research, I’m usually doing something with a PC or my 3D printer, playing guitar, traveling, or exploring new coffee shops.

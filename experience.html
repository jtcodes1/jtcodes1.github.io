LOS ALAMOS NATIONAL LABORATORY · T-1

Scientific Computing Research Intern

Theoretical Division · On-site / Remote
JUN 2026 – PRESENT

At Los Alamos National Laboratory's T-1 group, I design original GPU algorithms for molecular simulation. I developed ALAMOS and ALAMOS-0, demonstrated 200,000 stable MD steps across 104,640 atoms without corrective Krylov iterations, and am bringing these methods into native C++/MPI LAMMPS software. I'm first author of our 13-author Shadow MD paper, available on arXiv, with coauthors at Los Alamos and Sandia National Laboratories. I'm also preparing a second first-author ALAMOS methods manuscript while finishing a 4.0-GPA CS degree at DePaul.

  • Derived and GPU-implemented charge-conserving PPCG. At 10⁻³ RMS, PPCG + full ALAMOS averaged 1.0 iteration per MD step versus 12.9 with GMRES + diagonal (13× fewer); at the stricter 10⁻⁵ RMS tolerance, they were 3.06 versus 23.47 (87% fewer).
  • Invented ALAMOS, a multiscale approximate inverse for charge response, reducing strict-tolerance solver iterations by 87% versus GMRES + diagonal (23.47 → 3.06 per step with PPCG + full ALAMOS); advanced it to ALAMOS-0, eliminating 100% of corrective Krylov iterations across the tested 200,000-step, 104,640-atom trajectory.
  • Ran a 104,640-atom, 200,000-step NVE simulation on an NVIDIA A100 with zero Krylov corrections and charge-dependent calculations accounting for approximately 8.4% of runtime. Am integrating native C++ shadow MD and ALAMOS-0 into LAMMPS, with initial serial/MPI validation complete.
  • Implemented the GJ-I Langevin integrator and ran 1.1 ns trajectories at 100 K and 300 K. I analyzed the temperature distributions against Gamma statistics and computed IR spectra from dipole autocorrelations, comparing to converged charge references and experimental water bands.
  • First-authored a 13-author paper available on arXiv:2611.03499 through a LANL–Sandia research collaboration; I'm actively preparing my second first-author ALAMOS methods manuscript, with a third follow-on paper planned. Presented a T-1 Research Lightning Talk (July 30, 2026) and a separate poster at the LANL Student Symposium (August 4, 2026).
